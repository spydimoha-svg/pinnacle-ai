Protecting Pinnacle

You asked how to patent the website. The honest answer is that you almost certainly cannot patent it in India — and you should not want to. Here is what the law actually allows, what it costs, what order to do it in, and what genuinely protects this business instead.

Start here — Section 3(k)

Under Section 3(k) of the Patents Act 1970, a "computer programme per se", an algorithm, a mathematical method and a business method are not inventions at all in Indian law. They are not merely hard to patent — they are excluded from the definition before novelty is even considered.

A website, an app, a UI, a tutoring workflow and a subscription model all sit squarely inside that exclusion. There is no filing strategy that gets around it for a product shaped like Pinnacle.

01

When software can be patented here

rarely worth it

The word "per se" leaves a narrow opening. The Patent Office's revised CRI Guidelines (2025) ask examiners to look for a technical effect: does the software materially change how the hardware performs, and does it let the machine do something it otherwise could not? Accepted examples are things like a genuine improvement in processing speed, network communication, memory use, or security at the system level.

Claim you might wantOutcomeWhy
"A cinematic AI tutoring interface"RefusedPresentation of information + programme per se.
"A method of grading CBSE answers with AI"RefusedAlgorithm and business/teaching method.
"A subscription model for school licensing"RefusedBusiness method, explicitly excluded.
"A knowledge-graph data structure for curricula"RefusedAbstract data organisation, no technical effect.
A novel on-device inference technique that measurably cuts memory on low-end hardwareArguableReal technical effect on the machine — but you would have to have actually invented it.

Even where a claim is arguable, weigh the cost. A drafted and prosecuted Indian software patent runs roughly ₹1.5–3 lakh and three to five years, and publication at 18 months hands your method to every competitor whether or not the patent is ever granted. For a pre-revenue product that money buys about eight months of runway instead.

Watch out for this

A number of Indian filing agencies will happily take ₹50,000+ to file a "software patent" they know will be refused under 3(k), because they are paid to file, not to succeed. If a service promises to patent your website, that is the tell. Ask them directly, in writing, how they intend to overcome Section 3(k). A real attorney will explain the technical-effect test; a mill will change the subject.

02

Copyright — you already have it

₹500–2,000 per work

Software is a literary work under the Copyright Act 1957. Copyright exists automatically the moment you write the code — there is nothing to file for it to exist. Registration is optional, and its value is purely evidential: a certificate is far easier to put in front of a court or a platform than a git history.

  1. File Form XIV online at copyright.gov.in, under Rule 70(1) of the Copyright Rules 2013.
  2. Attach the source code: the first ten and last ten pages, unredacted. If the work is under twenty pages, the whole thing. Blocked-out portions get the application objected to.
  3. Pay ₹500 per work as an individual (₹2,000 if you file as a company or LLP).
  4. Receive a Diary Number, then wait out the 30-day objection window.
  5. Certificate issues in roughly 2–8 months if nobody objects.

03

Trademark — the one that actually matters

₹4,500 per class

This is where your money should go first. A patent protects a method you probably do not have; a trademark protects the name customers will eventually search for, and it is the thing a school's procurement form and any future investor will ask about.

ClassCoversFeePriority
Class 41Education and training services — tutoring, coaching, course delivery₹4,500File now
Class 9Downloadable software and mobile apps₹4,500File now
Class 42SaaS platform, hosted software, software development₹4,500Add when funded
  1. Search first, free, on the IP India public search. If "Pinnacle" is taken in Class 41 by an education business, you need to know before you print anything.
  2. Get a Startup India recognition certificate if you qualify — it keeps you at the ₹4,500 small-entity rate rather than ₹9,000, and unlocks fee rebates elsewhere.
  3. File TM-A online for Class 41 and Class 9. Use the word mark and the logo if the logo is settled.
  4. Use ™ immediately on filing. You may only use ® once registered.
  5. Wait it out: examination takes 6–12 months, then a 4-month opposition window. Uncontested registration runs 12–18 months.

Do not file only Class 41. Protecting the teaching service but not the software leaves an enforcement gap that cannot be fixed retroactively — only by filing a fresh application in the missing class, from the back of the queue. Expedited examination exists (Form TM-M, ₹20,000 for a small entity) but is rarely worth it pre-revenue.

04

Trade secret — free, and your strongest protection

₹0

India has no standalone trade-secret statute; protection comes through contract and confidentiality. That sounds weak and is actually the most practical defence you have, because the things that make Pinnacle good are exactly the things you never have to publish.

  • Keep the prompt architecture private. The persona contract, the lesson phase machine, the marking prompts. A patent would publish these; secrecy does not.
  • Keep the corpus private. Authored concept maps, probes with stored answers, examiner keywords. Serve them, never ship them as a downloadable file.
  • NDAs before demos with schools and any partner who sees the internals.
  • Written IP assignment from every contributor — freelancer, friend, intern. Without it they own their contribution, and this is the single most common way small companies discover they do not own their own product.
  • Never commit secrets and keep the private repo private.

The thing worth internalising

Your defensibility is not legal. It is the authored CBSE corpus — verbatim NCERT, real marking schemes, and above all the teacher-corrected answer scripts your OSM console will accumulate. A competitor can copy your UI in a fortnight and your prompts in a month. They cannot copy years of marked scripts from real classrooms, and no patent would have protected that anyway.

Spend ₹11,000 on the trademark and copyrights, skip the patent, and put the ₹2 lakh you would have burned on filings into authoring more chapters.

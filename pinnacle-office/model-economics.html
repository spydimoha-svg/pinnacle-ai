The one thing to fix first

Licensing risk — blocks launch

Google's Gemini free tier explicitly excludes commercial use, and trains on free-tier prompts. Pinnacle currently runs on free keys (see api/_llm.ts). That is fine while nobody pays you. The moment you charge a student or a school, the free tier is being used to run a revenue-generating service — outside its terms — and your students' questions are training data.

Groq, Cerebras and OpenRouter free tiers carry comparable restrictions. Budget for a paid key before the first invoice, not after. The good news is below: at these token prices it costs almost nothing.

What one student actually costs

Modelled on Pinnacle's own request shape: roughly 3,500 input tokens per turn (persona, the 6,000-character grounding block, ten turns of history) and ~600 output against the 1,600 ceiling in api/_llm.ts. A committed student runs about 300 turns a month — 15 a day, 20 days. That is 1.05M input and 0.18M output tokens monthly.

Two levers that cut this further

Prompt caching. Your grounding block is identical for every student on the same chapter — about 1,500 of those 3,500 input tokens. Cached input runs roughly 90% cheaper on Gemini, OpenAI and Anthropic, which takes Flash-Lite from ₹15.6 to about ₹11. This is the single highest-return change available, and it is a routing change, not a rewrite.

Batch API, 50% off. Applies to anything not waiting on a student: worksheet generation, video scripts, the OSM first-pass marking when a teacher queues a whole class overnight. Not for live tutoring.

What to charge, and what you keep

The market reference: Physics Wallah sells batches at ₹2,000–5,000 one-time, and its Vi bundle runs ₹375 per 28 days. Byju's is gone — insolvent, delisted — which is why the mid-market is open. Price under PW's bundle and you are the obvious second subscription a serious student holds.

The margin you actually need

Target 70% gross and you are safe; you will clear 85%. Tokens are not your cost problem and will not become one. The two things that will eat this margin are the ones not in the table: acquiring each student, and answering the ones who write in. Hold customer acquisition under ₹450 a head — roughly one and a half months of a Standard plan — and every student is profitable inside the first quarter.

Reinvestment, and the machine

You said the profit splits two ways: back into the next business, and into hardware you can build models on. Here is what each side needs.

Option VRAM / memory Bandwidth Street price What it lets you do
RTX 5090 build 32 GB GDDR7 1,792 GB/s ≈ ₹4.5L Recommended Fine-tune up to ~30B comfortably. 4× faster on anything that fits. This is the right first machine — Pinnacle's own models are small.
NVIDIA DGX Spark 128 GB unified 273 GB/s ≈ ₹4.1L Loads 70B–200B models, but decodes far slower. Buy this only when capacity genuinely beats speed.

At the Standard plan you keep about ₹266 gross per student per month. A ₹4.5L machine is therefore 1,700 student-months — 140 paying students for a year, or 280 for six months. That is a realistic first-year target for one school district, and it means the hardware is funded by the product rather than by savings.

  • Split the profit 60/30/10. 60% into the next business, 30% held for the machine until it is bought outright, 10% as a buffer for the quarter a model provider changes its prices on you.
  • Do not buy the box early. Until you have a dataset only you own — and marked answer scripts from the OSM console are exactly that — a rented GPU hour is cheaper than an owned one that idles.
  • Your moat is the corpus, not the model. Verbatim NCERT, real marking schemes, and teacher-corrected scripts are the asset. Any model can be swapped; that data cannot be bought.

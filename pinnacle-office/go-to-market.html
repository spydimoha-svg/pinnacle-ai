Marketing playbook · 90-day sequence · 19 Aug 2026

How to sell Pinnacle

A step-by-step launch sequence built around one advantage most edtech founders do not have: you built a tool teachers want before you built the thing students pay for. That inverts the entire acquisition problem, and this playbook is organised around exploiting it.

The wedge — everything below depends on this

Every consumer edtech in India is fighting for the same parent's attention with the same paid ads, and losing money doing it. You have a second door: the on-screen marking console. A CBSE teacher marks 40 scripts a term and hates every hour of it. Give that away free, forever, to any teacher who wants it.

One principal saying yes puts Pinnacle in front of 200–2,000 students at effectively zero acquisition cost, with the school's own credibility attached. That is the only channel in Indian edtech whose economics actually work, and it is the one nobody else can copy quickly because they did not build the teacher tool.

00

Fix what blocks the sale

Week 0 · before any outreach
Do not market until these three are done

1. Move off free LLM tiers. Gemini's free tier bars commercial use and trains on your prompts. The day you take money you are in breach, and a school's IT head will ask where student data goes.

2. Write a privacy policy that names minors. You are handling data from children under 18. Schools ask for this in the first meeting; not having it ends the meeting.

3. File the trademark. Not because someone will steal the name this month, but because a school procurement form asks for it and because filing is cheap now and expensive later.

Write the one sentence first

Before any asset, settle the positioning. Not a tagline — a claim you can defend in a staffroom. Something in the shape of:

Positioning Pinnacle marks your class's answer scripts in a third of the time, and teaches the students who got them wrong — grounded in the actual NCERT text and the real marking scheme, so it never invents an exercise number.

Note what that sentence does: it leads with the teacher's pain, and the student product arrives as a consequence. Every asset you make should follow that order.

01

Ten students before one rupee of ads

Weeks 1–3

Proof first. You need evidence a stranger will believe, and you cannot buy that.

  1. Recruit 10 students by hand. Your school, your neighbourhood, relatives' children. Free. In exchange they agree to be studied.
  2. Record a real before-and-after. A diagnostic score at the start, the same paper six weeks later. This single number is your most valuable marketing asset for the next two years.
  3. Sit and watch three of them use it. Not a survey — watch. Every place they hesitate is a place a school demo will fail.
  4. Get two teachers marking with the OSM console. Time them against marking by hand. "Cut marking time from 4 hours to 90 minutes" is the line that opens school doors.
  5. Collect three quotes with full names and schools attached. Anonymous testimonials convince nobody in this market.

Gate: do not move to Phase 02 until you can state one honest outcome number and one honest time-saved number. If six weeks produced neither, the product is not ready and marketing will only make that fact travel faster.

02

The school channel

Weeks 3–10 · the main engine

This is where the business is won. Target CBSE-affiliated private schools with 500–2,000 students in tier-2 and tier-3 cities — big enough to have a budget, small enough that the principal decides alone and you are not stuck in a procurement committee for nine months.

  1. Build a list of 50 schools. The CBSE affiliation directory is public. Record the principal's name and the head of science or maths.
  2. Lead with the teacher tool, never the subscription. The first email offers free marking software for their staff. Nothing else. No pricing, no student plans.
  3. Ask for 20 minutes with two teachers, not the principal. Teachers are easier to reach, and a teacher who loves it will sell the principal for you — internally, which is worth more than anything you can say.
  4. Demo by marking their own paper. Bring nothing generic. Ask for a real answer script beforehand and mark it live. This is the whole pitch.
  5. Leave the teachers using it for a full term. Free. Do not sell yet. You are buying proof and habit.
  6. Return with usage data. "Your staff marked 340 scripts and saved an estimated 96 hours." Now propose student licences at the bulk rate.
  7. Price per student, per year, invoiced to the school. Parents paying individually is a collections problem; a school paying once is a customer.
First email · keep it this short Dear [Name] — I've built a marking tool for CBSE teachers that cuts script marking to about a third of the time, and I'm giving it to a few schools free this term to see how it holds up in real use. Could I show two of your maths or science teachers, for twenty minutes, at whatever time suits them? No cost and nothing to install. — Ayaan, Pinnacle AI

Expect roughly 5 replies from 50 emails, 2 demos, 1 school. That is a normal, healthy funnel. One school in ten weeks is not a slow start — it is a repeatable motion, and the second one is much faster than the first.

03

The content engine

Weeks 4 onward · runs forever

Word-of-mouth is the only edtech acquisition channel in India whose cost is falling, and content-led acquisition runs roughly 68% cheaper than paid. You have an unfair advantage here that is worth stating plainly: your product manufactures your marketing. The video studio generates narrated, NCERT-grounded lessons with 3D figures. That is the content.

The one rule for content

Every piece teaches something complete on its own. No "download the app to see the answer." That tactic buys one click and burns the trust that makes the channel work at all. The product is the follow-on, never the paywall.

04

Paid — only after organic works

Month 3+, conditional

Do not open an ad account until organic is converting. Paid amplifies a working message; it cannot find one for you, and finding one with paid is how edtech companies burn their runway.

  • Start at ₹500/day, one channel, one message. Meta, retargeting people who watched a full YouTube lesson. Warmest possible audience.
  • Advertise the free thing, not the paid thing. The worksheet generator or a chapter video. Sell the subscription later, in-product.
  • Hard ceiling: ₹450 CAC. That is roughly 1.5 months of a Standard plan. Above it, stop the campaign the same week — do not "give it time".
  • Never pay for keywords the coaching giants own. "JEE preparation" is a money fire. "Class 10 marking scheme" is not.
05

Make students bring students

Month 3 onward
  1. Give a free month for a friend who stays 30 days. Reward retention, not signups, or you will buy a pile of dead accounts.
  2. Make progress worth showing. The end-of-chapter summary — concepts mastered, misconceptions corrected — should be one tap to share. Students share evidence of getting better; they do not share ads.
  3. Recruit 10 teachers as paid ambassadors. A small per-school commission. A teacher recommending you is worth more than any influencer in this market.
  4. Ask at the moment of success only. Prompt for a referral right after a student clears a trial they previously failed — never on a schedule.

The one number that decides everything: week-4 retention. At 85–89% gross margin, a retained student is enormously profitable and almost any CAC is recoverable. If students leave in month one, no marketing spend will ever work, and every rupee spent before fixing it is wasted twice — once on the ad, once on the reputation.
